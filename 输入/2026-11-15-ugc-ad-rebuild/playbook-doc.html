7 Steps to Clone Any Winning UGC Ad for Your Product With AI...

Most AI UGC starts from a prompt, and a prompt can only describe a video.

A clone keeps the structure of an ad that already works and swaps in everything that is yours, so the hook, the beats and the cuts stay, and the person, the product and the words are new.

The top row is 6 real ads. The bottom row is our 6 clones, all made with 1 skill.

None of them cost more than about $7.50, retries included.

This guide breaks down the 7 steps behind them, what each step produced in our runs, and what went wrong.

1. Read the Reference

What it is: Two readers go through the ad: frames pulled with ffmpeg, and a Gemini video reader that watches with sound.

Why it matters: Your agent cannot play a video or hear it, and a guess at this step is a fabrication.

How to do it:

  • pull 4 frames per second for the beats, and 10 per second around a suspected cut
  • let the reader draft the actions and let the frames decide, because its timestamps drift by about half a second
  • take words, music and sound from the reader's transcript only

Tip: On the romper ad the reader said 2 shots. The frames said 4. Frames win.

2. Write the Target

What it is: A swap map from their creator, product and room to yours, plus 1 target sheet per clip that says what the viewer must see.

Why it matters: Skip it and the swap happens silently inside each prompt, and whatever you forgot is filled in by the model.

How to do it:

  • list every element of the ad next to what replaces it: product, person, clothes, place, light, caption
  • write every row as a fact you can check in a frame
  • never leave "must not appear" empty, and start it with everything the reference would leak

"Looks natural" is not a row.

Then ask whether your product can do what theirs does.

3. Plan the Clips

What it is: Where to cut, how long each clip runs, how hard each fact needs to be pinned down, and the budget.

Why it matters: A video model makes 1 continuous take from 1 set of inputs, and the ad is several shots.

How to do it:

  • end a clip at every hard cut, and give each new shot its own start frame
  • generate the shot plus 0.5-1 s of handle, because the first and last half-second are the least reliable
  • when a fact fails, climb 1 rung of the constraint ladder and never retry the same rung with new words

You confirm the target sheets, the clip plan and the budget together.

4. Build Start Frames

What it is: 1 confirmed image per clip that already shows its first frame: person, wardrobe, product state, pose, room, framing.

Why it matters: A start frame costs about $0.13 and a clip starts at $0.55, so this is where facts get settled for cents instead of dollars.

How to do it:

  • edit the reference shot's first frame when the pose must be exact
  • generate a new image when the pose is loose and the room or outfit matter most
  • check image against image, with the reference frame beside the start frame and every row marked pass or fail

Wardrobe, room, framing and a product that is open, worn or hidden all need a start frame, because text alone is never enough.

5. Build the Video Inputs

What it is: For each clip you choose what to bind, audit it against the target sheet, write the prompt and get the quote.

Why it matters: Each image, clip and sentence is a vote, and 2 inputs that disagree are settled by the stronger one.

How to do it:

  • remove every input that contradicts the target for this clip
  • make sure every row of the target sheet has an input that shows it
  • let the prompt add only what no image can: action, physics, camera, negatives, sound

The quote comes last.

6. Generate and Check

What it is: Clip 1 is generated and checked first, because its result tests the plan. The others follow once it passes.

Why it matters: A check without the target sheet once said "no problems" about a clip where the bra was worn over the t-shirt.

How to do it:

  • compare frame 0 with the start frame first, because a different frame 0 is a fail whatever happens later
  • discard any clip that shows the reference creator's face, hair, garments or room
  • tick every target row from 4-per-second tiles, with the reference above and yours below

Note: Name the cause before you change anything. Rewording the prompt is not a repair.

7. Post-Production

What it is: The passed clips become 2 files: the ad, and a comparison video with the reference and the clone on 1 timeline.

Why it matters: You judge a clone at the same second, not from memory.

How to do it:

  • land every cut where the reference cuts, by moving the trim window and never the reference timing
  • add on-screen text here and never generate it inside a clip
  • check that each cut sits within about 0.2 s of the reference's

On the gym vlog every cut landed within 0.06 s of the ad's.

Mistakes That Kill a Clone

We have 17 failures on record. Here are 4 of them, and the 1 fix that never works.

A product photo that contradicts the shot

In 1 clip the bra is hidden under a t-shirt, and it came back worn on top from the first frame.

The product photo was still bound. Bind the start frame only.

2 good frames no camera move can connect

The legs sat on top of the bags in the first frame and under them in the last, so the clip dissolved halfway.

Take both frames from 1 wide image, so only a pan separates them.

Wardrobe that only exists in the text

The t-shirt got lifted to the chest or drawn as a crop top. Wardrobe always comes from a start frame.

A long take with the motion left to text

A 6-second clip held 1 clean hip thrust for 3 seconds, then drifted.

Keep the first 3 seconds and generate a 4-second clip from that exact frame.

Rewording the prompt

Rewording is not a repair.

Name the cause first: a missing input, a contradicting input, a constraint that is too weak, a wrong target, or a model limit.

Tip: Keep every past failure on a list and replay it before any rule changes, so no earlier fix gets undone.
