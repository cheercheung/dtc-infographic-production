realism in AI UGC comes down to 4 things:

- the face
- the voice
- the environment
- and the motion

get all 4 right and nobody can tell it's AI.

this is the full breakdown of each one…

## why ads get clocked as ai:

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HWrL6eLWEAAi73K?format=jpg&name=large)

before the how, understand what the viewer is reacting to.

they feel something is off in the first second, and they scroll.

the 4 things causing that feeling:

**1\. the face is generic.**

they've seen that same AI face 40 times this week, because most models are trained on similar data.

**2\. the voice and the room don't match.**

a clean studio voice coming from someone standing in a kitchen.

**3\. the person doesn't move like a person.**

no gestures, no blinking, perfectly even pacing.

**4\. the setting doesn't match the claim.**

a skincare review filmed in an office.

every one of these is fixable, and each has its own method.

## part 1: the face

**stop prompting faces from scratch.**

"pretty girl, brown hair, 25" gives you the same generic face every model makes.

**the method that works:**

- describe a specific person: age, face shape, skin texture, small imperfections
- build a character sheet in MakeUGC: front, side, back, full body + close-up
- lock in the details: jewelry, hair, glasses, outfit

**why this works.**

the model now knows exactly who this person is from every angle, so the face stays the same in every clip.

**then match the avatar to the claim.**

a 22-year-old talking about joint pain fails before the script even starts.

the age, look and setting have to make the person believable as someone with that problem.

build a library of 4–5 characters covering different ages and looks, and reuse them across campaigns.

## part 2: the voice

this is the part that gives an ad away fastest, and the one people think about least.

**the problem.**

a clean studio voice in a kitchen gets clocked before anyone even looks at the face.

**what solves it.**

Seedance 2.5 on MakeUGC generates the voice inside the scene itself, so it carries the room:

- a voice in a car carries the cabin tone
- a voice in a bathroom carries the echo
- a voice in a big room carries the space

no separate voice tool and no syncing afterwards. the voice and the room come out matching.

writing for a human read.

write the script with the imperfections in:

half sentences, an "honestly…", a small restart.

perfectly smooth delivery reads as generated, no matter how good the model is.

read it out loud before you generate.

anything you wouldn't say to a friend in the car gets rewritten.

## part 3: the environment

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/amplify_video_thumb/2119734127222001664/img/gNRsCtR5uQo5fe_q.jpg?name=large)

the setting does 2 jobs.

it makes the claim believable, and it decides how well the model renders.

matching setting to claim:

- a car reads as private and unscripted
- a bathroom or bedroom suits anything personal
- a kitchen suits food, supplements and anything domestic
- a store aisle looks like someone caught mid-shop
- an office or desk suits B2B and software

a skincare review in a bathroom lands.

the same script in an office looks staged.

the technical reason some settings render better.

a car interior is small, contained and evenly lit.

fewer surfaces, fewer moving objects and a fixed camera mean fewer failed generations.

**keeping the setting consistent.**

the character sheet holds the face, but you have to check the room yourself.

make sure the lighting, setting and camera angle stay the same across every scene.

## part 4: the motion

this is the layer people skip, and it's where the last tells hide.

**check before anything ships:**

- eye contact holds on camera, no drifting mid-sentence
- hands move, because real people gesture while they talk
- pacing has small stumbles, not perfect evenness
- blinks look natural
- the background has some movement where the setting implies it

## a few quick notes:

1\. not every generation is a keeper. expect to generate more than you need.

2\. then test: \[your daily budget per creative\], judge CTR first and hold rate second, and cut what isn't working after \[X\] days.

one thing to understand before you optimise for realism.

the ads that print are often not the most realistic ones.

claymation, cartoon and story formats regularly beat a perfect talking head,

because attention is the real bottleneck. believability is just the entry ticket.

realism stops an ad from being dismissed. it's not what makes it convert.
